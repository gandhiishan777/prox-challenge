# Maintenance

> **WARNING:** TO PREVENT SERIOUS INJURY, FIRE AND BURNS:
> Unplug the Welder, rest the tool on a heat-proof, electrically non-conductive surface, and allow all parts of the Welder to cool thoroughly before service.

1. **BEFORE EACH USE,** inspect the general condition of the Welder. Check for:
   - loose hardware
   - misalignment or binding of moving parts
   - damaged cord/electrical wiring
   - frayed or damaged cables
   - cracked or broken parts
   - any other condition that may affect its safe operation.

2. **PERIODICALLY,** have a qualified technician remove the Rear Panel and use compressed air to blow out all dust from the interior.

3. **AFTER EVERY USE**, store in a clean and dry location.

4. **For optimal MIG/Flux-Cored wire weld quality, clean and inspect the MIG Gun Contact Tip and Nozzle before each use, as explained below.**

## MIG Gun Nozzle and Contact Tip Inspection and Cleaning

1. **Make sure that the entire MIG Gun is completely cool and that the Power Cord is unplugged from the electrical outlet before proceeding.**
2. Pull the Nozzle to remove it.
3. Scrub the interior of the Nozzle clean with a wire brush.
4. Examine the end of the Nozzle. The end should be flat and even. If the end is uneven, chipped, melted, cracked, or otherwise damaged, the Nozzle will adversely effect the weld and should be replaced.
5. Unscrew the Contact Tip counterclockwise and slide it off the welding wire to remove.
6. Scrub the outside of the Tip clean with a wire brush. Clean out the inside of the tip with a tip cleaner (sold separately). Check that the Tip is the proper type for the wire size used.
7. Examine the shape of the hole at the end of the Contact Tip. It should be an even circle; it should not be oblong or have any bulges in it.
8. If any problems are noted, replace the Contact Tip. Select a new Tip of the correct size for the welding wire used.
9. Reinstall the Tip and securely reinstall the Nozzle as well.

# Troubleshooting–MIG/Flux-Cored Welding

**IMPORTANT!**
**Be CERTAIN to shut off the Welder, disconnect it from power, and discharge the MIG Gun to ground before adjusting, cleaning, or repairing the unit.**

| Problem | Possible Causes | Likely Solutions |
|---|---|---|
| Wire Feed Motor Runs but Wire Does Not Feed Properly | 1. Insufficient wire feed pressure.<br>2. Incorrect wire feed roller size.<br>3. Damaged MIG Gun, cable, or liner assembly.<br>4. Feed Tensioner is too tight. | 1. Increase wire feed pressure properly. Follow step 27 on page 17.<br>2. Flip roll to correct size. Follow the Feed Roller instructions on page 12.<br>3. Have a qualified technician inspect these parts and replace as necessary.<br>4. Loosen Feed Tensioner so it applies only enough pressure to prevent continued spinning after the Gun Trigger is released. |
| Wire Creates a Bird's Nest During Operation | 1. Excess wire feed pressure.<br>2. Incorrect Contact Tip size.<br>3. MIG Gun Cable Connector not fully inserted into Wire Feed mechanism.<br>4. Damaged liner. | 1. Adjust wire feed pressure properly. Follow step 27 on page 17.<br>2. Replace with the proper tip for wire used.<br>3. Insert Gun Cable Connector properly. Follow steps 13 and 14 on page 13.<br>4. Have a qualified technician inspect and repair/replace as necessary. |
| Wire Stops During Welding | 1. Gun cable is severely bent and Wire Feed mechanism cannot feed wire.<br>2. Gun liner is clogged or worn.<br>3. Gun liner is too small for welding wire being used.<br>4. Wire is tangled on the spool.<br>5. Wire is not making contact with Feed Rollers.<br>6. Feed Roller is not making enough contact with wire or is crushing flux-cored wire. | 1. Straighten Gun cable.<br>2. Check gun liner for obstruction. Replace if necessary.<br>3. | Welding Arc Not Stable | 1. Wire not feeding properly.<br>2. Incorrect Contact Tip or liner size or excessive wear.<br>3. Incorrect wire feed speed.<br>4. Loose MIG Gun cable or ground cable.<br>5. See first Troubleshooting section above.<br>2. Replace with the proper tip or liner size for wire used.<br>3. Adjust wire feed speed to achieve a more stable arc.<br>4. Ensure polarity is correct for operation: DCEP for MIG welding and DCEN for Flux-Cored self-shielded welding.<br>7. Ensure gas flow rate is set according to Settings Chart. | Weak Arc Strength | 1. Incorrect line voltage.<br>2. Improper gauge or length of cord.<br>3. Not enough current. | 1. Check the line voltage and, if insufficient, have a licensed electrician remedy the situation.<br>2. Do not use an extension cord on this Welder. Use only one of the supplied power cords for this Welder or an identical replacement cord.<br>3. Switch current to proper setting for metal thickness. |

# Wiring Schematic

[FIGURE wiring-schematic | bbox: 70,120,1160,1600 | Full electrical schematic of the welder: AC input through the PFC and rectifier stages, the IGBT inverter bridge and output transformer feeding OUT+ / OUT−, and the MCU board's connectors driving the LCD screen, wire feeder motor, gas solenoid valves, fans and remote board.]

The schematic is a single sheet. Reading it by functional block:

**Output side (top left).** Terminals **OUT+** and **OUT−** are drawn at the top.
The OUT+ leg passes through a **Hall** current sensor and an output inductor. A
bank of output rectifier diodes (eight in parallel across the secondary) and
snubber capacitors sit between the transformer secondary and the output
terminals. Connector **CN1** attaches on the left of this block.

**Inverter and transformer (center left).** Four **IGBT** switches are drawn in a
full-bridge arrangement feeding the main transformer's primary. The transformer
(marked T60*30*28) has its secondary tapped to the output rectifier block above.

**PFC stage (center).** A block labelled **PFC** with its own **IGBT** drives the
power factor correction inductor, fed from the input **RECTIFIER** blocks (two
bridge rectifiers drawn side by side, each with pins 1–4).

**Input (bottom left).** Marked **K1 AC 120-240V / 50/60HZ**, with terminals
**AC1**, **AC2** and **G** (ground). An auxiliary supply transformer with
filtering feeds the low-voltage rails; a **24V** rail is labelled near the
control circuitry.

**MCU BOARD (center right).** The main control board, drawn as a large block with
three gate-drive transformer symbols inside it. Its connectors:
- **CN5** and **CN7** — the wide ribbon headers (pins numbered 1 through 34) that
  carry the control/feedback bus to the power section on the left.
- **CN6** — to the **LCD SCREEN** block (drawn upper right, pins 1–4).
- **CN3** — to the **FAST WIRE FEED SWITCH** and on to a **SOLENOID VALVE**.
- **CN4** — to the **WIRE FEEDER** motor, drawn as a block marked **M**.
- **CN2** — down to the **REMOTE BOARD**.
- **CN8** — to the remote board's connector **CN1**.

**Peripherals (bottom right).** The **REMOTE BOARD** block carries connectors
CN1, CN2 and CN3, which run out to two **AVIATION PLUG** connectors (these are
the foot pedal and wire feed control sockets). Two **SOLENOID VALVE** blocks are
drawn above it for gas control.

**Cooling (bottom).** **FAN** and **FAN2**, each a two-pin connector, drive the
two cooling fans drawn as large squares with X's across them at the bottom right.
